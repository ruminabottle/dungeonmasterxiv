# Rolls: product spec

Describes the current product. Converted 2026-09-29 from `PRD-2-rolls.md`, plus the brief's
"Reference: Foundry VTT chat" section and the resolved escalations that bear on it; the original is
archived at `~/archive/dungeonmasterxiv-agent-team-2026-09-29/claude/team/product/prd/PRD-2-rolls.md`.

Principles are cited as `product-overview D-n` and session requirements as `session-layer R-1.x`;
neither is restated here. The panel's thirteen shaping decisions are listed in product-overview
"Session panel" and cited as `product-overview Session panel item n`.

## Purpose

This area is the session panel: a shared chat that carries rolls, and the log it leaves. It answers
the brief's first problem (rolls stop scrolling away: every roll is captured, attributed and kept in
order on every admitted client) and its sixth (detail at hand when a roll lands).

The build order is the user's: **base chat first, so people in a session can talk; then a dice
command on top of it.** The order governs what a user can do and when they get it. It does not reach
code no user can reach: a pure evaluator (text in, a result or a named refusal out, with no panel,
wiring or transport) does not make the product roll-first, because nobody can use it. Once such a
piece gains a surface a user can reach, the order applies to it in full.

Two facts shape everything below, and they pull in opposite directions on purpose. **The roller's own
client produces the roll**, which keeps the product from becoming an authority over the table. **The
host orders it**, which keeps everyone looking at the same log. Requirements describe what a person
can do and what the product must not claim; where one names a screen, that is a defect in the
requirement. The input surface is engineering's.

**Rolls are generated by the plugin, and the plugin reads no game chat.** Three reasons, in order of
weight:

1. The game cannot roll what tabletop needs. `/dice 20` gives one die and one number; it cannot
   express `4d6+2`.
2. It removes localized parsing entirely. Reading `/random` results means parsing localized
   `SeString` across EN/DE/FR/JP clients, and an English DM with a German player is ordinary in EU
   roleplay. That class of fragility is absent rather than handled.
3. Presentation becomes the product's own.

**The cost, accepted knowingly:** FFXIV's `/random` is server-generated and cannot be faked. A plugin
roll is generated on the roller's machine, so a modified client could announce anything and nothing
downstream could detect it. Tabletop runs on trust, and a group wanting an unfakeable roll can still
type `/dice`; the plugin will not know about it. See R-2.16.

## Requirements

### R-2.1 Composing a roll

- A participant composes a roll in the plugin and sends it to the session.
- **The grammar is the full Foundry VTT dice grammar:** pools, success counting, comparisons,
  keep/drop, exploding dice, rerolls, labelled terms, and nesting. `d20` means `1d20`; `(1d8+4)*2` is
  valid. This was chosen over a smaller grammar knowingly.
- **Malformed notation is refused with a message naming what was wrong. It never silently rolls
  something else.**
- **A modifier does not bind to its term across a space (U+0020).** Foundry's published modifier
  pattern ("anything until a space, group symbol, or arithmetic operator") cannot capture a space, for
  any modifier (`k`, `r`, `x`, `d`, a bare comparison), and it is unchanged across major versions.
  So `2d6 d20` is not `2d6` with a drop modifier. What the whole input should evaluate as (two terms,
  an implicit operator, or a refusal) is not established. **Pending that, refuse:** an expression the
  product cannot confidently place in the grammar must not be confidently answered. Refusing
  something Foundry accepts is a lapse the user sees and can work around; silently answering
  something Foundry refuses is one they cannot.
- A roll may carry a short free-text label ("initiative", "perception", "damage") that the plugin
  stores and displays and **never interprets** (product-overview D-4).
- **Where D-4's line falls with a large grammar.** Counting successes against a number **the user
  typed** is arithmetic on dice and is in scope. Knowing what number to compare against is rules
  content and is not. The evaluator counting successes is arithmetic, not a resolution rule.
  **Success/failure resolution (the product deciding whether a roll succeeded) is deferred and must
  not arrive as a side effect of the grammar**: no target-number concept is built, and wiring the
  evaluator to a surface must not make that decision by connecting two existing pieces.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.2** Every construct in the grammar evaluates: pools, success counting against a typed number,
  comparisons, keep/drop, exploding, rerolls, labelled terms, and nesting. Each construct is a
  separate failure.
- **A-2.3** Malformed notation is refused with a message naming the fault, and no roll is recorded.
- **A-2.3b** Input that is accepted is never evaluated as something other than what the user wrote
  without saying so. **When dice were rolled and none survived (the dice list is non-empty and no
  entry was kept), the result says so in words, not only in markup, whatever the total.** `total=0`
  beside `[1*,6*]` fails; so does `4d6dl4+100`, which drops every die and totals an ordinary-looking
  100, and `4d6kh0`, which reaches the same state with no drop modifier. Survival is the test, not
  the total: `4d10>9` and `4d6-15` can total zero with every die kept and must not trigger it, and
  `2+2` rolls no dice and is not in scope.
- **A-2.3c** A modifier is not bound to its term across a space. `2d6 d20` does not evaluate as `2d6`
  with a drop modifier; refusing it passes, and so does treating it as two terms. **The die count is
  the discriminator, not the total:** the drop reading rolls two dice and sets both aside, while a
  two-term reading of `2d6+d20` rolls three. This covers every modifier. Only U+0020 is established:
  `d 20` breaks the binding and `d\t20` does not, and tab, newline, CR and NBSP are not established,
  so tests pin the space cases and do not pin the others.

### R-2.1a A roll expression is untrusted input

Expressions arrive from other people. A peer's message can carry `999999d999999`, nesting hundreds
deep, or a pool that expands without bound.

- **Dice count, die size, nesting depth and total work are bounded**, and an expression exceeding any
  bound is refused with a message naming the fault.
- **A refusal is the required outcome.** A freeze, a hang or an out-of-memory is a failure of this
  requirement, not a performance issue.
- The bound values are engineering's; that bounds exist is not. The bounds are part of the evaluator
  from the start, not a later hardening pass: the input is untrusted the day it is wired.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.3a** A hostile expression is refused, not survived. `999999d999999`, deep nesting and an
  unbounded pool each return a message naming the fault. A run in which the client freezes, hangs or
  exhausts memory fails, including one that eventually recovers.

### R-2.2 Rolling

- **The roller's own client produces the roll and sends the result. Never the host.** A host that
  rolls for everyone becomes an authority this product refuses to build. This is a game among people
  who arranged to play together (session-layer R-1.3).
- The number comes from a cryptographically seeded generator, not a trivially predictable one. The
  result is not a secret; a predictable sequence would make the trust position worse than it needs to
  be.
- **Every die is reported individually and as a total.** This is an audit property, not a display
  nicety: a log showing totals alone is unfalsifiable, and no test or person could catch a bad roller.
- **A DM does not roll on behalf of a participant.** A DM rolling for an NPC is the DM's own roll and
  is ordinary; a roll attributed to a player but produced elsewhere is forbidden.
- **The plugin subscribes to no chat-read event at all.** "Not a chat logger" is a property of the
  build rather than a promise: there is no code path that reads what anyone says in game.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.1** `4d6+2` produces four individual die values in 1..6 and a correct total, and displays
  both. **The total is checked against something that is not the roller:** the displayed dice plus the
  modifier sum to the displayed total, asserted independently of the code that produced either. A
  test that asks the roller for both dice and total checks one computation against itself.
- **A-2.1a** The individual dice are shown, because that is what makes a roll auditable. A log
  showing totals alone fails.
- **A-2.4** Over a large sample, each face of a `d20` appears within expected bounds; the generator is
  not visibly skewed.
- **A-2.7** The built plugin contains no subscription to any chat-read event. Verified against source,
  as a standing property.
- **A-2.18** The individual dice are present in what the **recipient** receives, not only in what the
  roller displays. A build that sends the total and re-derives a plausible breakdown for display
  fails, and would pass any check made on the roller's own screen.

### R-2.3 The stream

**The panel shows one time-ordered stream containing messages, rolls and membership events** (joined,
left, dropped, reconnected), each timed.

- The roster shows who is here now; the stream shows when it changed. They answer different questions
  and neither replaces the other.
- **Admission mechanics stay out of the stream.** Who asked and who was refused belong to the
  admission flow (session-layer R-1.3). The stream records that membership
  changed, not how it was negotiated.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.8** A roll made by one participant appears on every entitled participant's screen within one
  frame of the host relaying it. Needs two machines.
- **A-2.27** A membership change appears in the stream, timed: joined, left, dropped, reconnected. A
  build showing only the current roster fails, and it is the build that looks correct on screen.

### R-2.4 Ordering and time

**The host sequences and timestamps everything. One order, one clock, identical logs.**

- Two clients never disagree about what happened first, and no client's local clock decides anything.
- This is what makes moderation, export and speaker attribution work (R-2.11, R-2.12, R-2.7).
  Without one order, a deleted message leaves a differently shaped hole on every screen.
- Host sequencing is not the relay deciding anything. The relay stays a pipe that stores nothing
  (product-overview D-2); the host is the participant already authoritative for shared state
  (product-overview D-3), and this uses that authority without extending it.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.5** Two clients fed the same events with different local timing show identical order and
  identical timestamps. A build in which any client's local clock reaches the log fails.

### R-2.5 Message kinds

**In-character, out-of-character, and emote**, with `/ooc` and `/me`.

- The three are distinguishable to a reader without relying on colour alone, because colour is the
  one channel a reader may not have.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.19** In-character, out-of-character and emote remain distinguishable with colour removed. A
  build distinguishing them by colour alone fails, and passes every check in the default palette. The
  non-colour signal is machine-checkable; a reader telling them apart needs the game.

### R-2.6 Targeting

**Two targets: everyone, and private to the DM** (either direction between a player and the DM).

- **Player-to-player privacy is deliberately not built.** FFXIV's `/tell` already does private
  player-to-player talk. What only this product offers is private talk that is part of the session
  record and can carry a roll. A worse `/tell` adds surface and competes with the game.
- **This is load-bearing on R-2.10, and neither may be revisited alone.** Holding messages for a
  dropped member is safe only because every message the host queues is one the host is already a
  legitimate party to. With player-to-player privacy the host would hold content it must not read,
  and R-2.10 would have to change in the same breath.
- A DM-private message is an access level under product-overview D-13: a non-recipient never
  receives it.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.15** A DM-private message's content is absent from a non-recipient's **received data**, not
  merely hidden in their view. Inspect what the client receives, not what it renders; a build that
  filters at the UI passes any screen-based check and fails this.

### R-2.7 Who a message is from

**Anyone may set a speaker.** The rendered form is `Character Name (Player Name)`: `Renn (DM)`,
`Eli (Tuka)`.

- **Speaking as yourself renders the person alone.** Never `Tuka (Tuka)`.
- **The parenthetical is never dropped from a rendered surface:** not from the echo, not on a narrow
  window. `Character (Player)` fits one line of plain text, so the echo carries it too.
- The reason is **attribution**: a reader needs to know who is speaking. It is not a defence against
  impersonation and never claimed to be one; the defence is the session role (R-2.7a).
- **The parenthetical is an aliasable display name (product-overview D-8) and authenticates nobody.**
  It is who the session knows you as. Copy never implies otherwise.
- "Never dropped" is about rendering. **An export carries no names at all** (product-overview D-20,
  R-2.12), so the parenthetical is required in the echo and forbidden in an export. An echo is a live
  render for someone already in the session, who can see the name in the panel a second later; an
  export is an artefact that leaves, and D-8 draws its line exactly there.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.20** Two participants sharing a display name are both usable in the panel, and neither is
  given an invented distinguishing suffix (session-layer R-1.3e). Both halves fail separately.
- **A-2.24** `Character (Player)` survives every rendered surface: the panel and the FFXIV echo. Each
  surface fails separately, and the echo is the one that will be dropped for width. The echo string is
  machine-checkable; the panel needs the game. It does not reach an export, where names are forbidden
  (A-2.17a).
- **A-2.25** Speaking as yourself renders the person alone. `Tuka (Tuka)` fails.

### R-2.7a The host is marked structurally, never by a name

**The session role is the defence: assigned by the session, never chosen by the sender.** It already
exists (Player, Assistant, Host) and is already rendered in the roster.

A name could never do this job. A member can set speaker `Renn` and display name `DM`, and a panel
relying on the parenthetical renders `Renn (DM)`: the defence rendering the impersonation. Display
names are aliasable and need not be distinct (session-layer R-1.3e).

- **The property, not negotiable: in every rendering, a member cannot produce a line
  indistinguishable from a host-authored one.** Reserve a token, render the host differently,
  anything; the mechanism is engineering's.
- **The echo is the hard case.** A one-line plain-text echo has no room for a badge, so it is where the
  marker gets dropped for width. A rendering that achieves the property in the panel and loses it in
  the echo has not achieved it.
- **Host specifically, not "anyone DM-ish".** The host is the sole author of shared state
  (product-overview D-3); an Assistant runs the table but is not that. Marking an Assistant as host
  makes the marker mean something vaguer than it claims.
- Open question: how the host's own speaker line renders is not decided. A parenthetical role such as
  `Renn (DM)` would contradict the one-meaning rule for parentheses below; a player's line keeps
  `Eli (Tuka)`, the person behind the speaker.
- **Parentheses carry exactly one meaning product-wide: the person behind the speaker.** The roster
  changes to make that true, not the message format: roles are not rendered in parentheses
  (session-layer R-1.3f, A-1.13d). A marker that must beat a convention every session reinforces only
  has to lose once.
- The reserved names (session-layer R-1.3j) close the obvious case and do not discharge this
  requirement at any list length.
- **Residual, stated rather than solved:** two players can still look alike. Display names collide by
  design; catching that is the DM's job at admission, and no rendering fixes it. No criterion may
  imply it is solved.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.24a** A member cannot produce a line indistinguishable from a host-authored one, **in the panel
  and in the echo**. Give a member an ordinary display name (`Tuka`), have them speak as `Renn`, and
  compare their rendered line with a genuine host line: the host's is distinguishable. The
  demonstration uses an ordinary name and no blocklist entry, so it is coupled to nothing: a build with
  the reserved-name list and no structural marker fails here. Both surfaces fail separately; the echo
  is the one that will fail.
- **A-2.24b** An Assistant is not marked as the host. A build treating anyone DM-ish as host fails.
- **A-2.24c** No copy states or implies that impersonation between players is prevented. Copy claiming
  players cannot be impersonated, are verified, or have unique names fails (session-layer R-1.7a).
  This bounds A-2.24a, and it can fail on its own.

### R-2.8 Inline rolls

- **`[[2d6]]`: immediate.** Evaluated as the message is sent, reading inline in the sentence.
- **`[[/roll 1d20]]`: clickable.** A formula any reader can act on to roll themselves; each person's
  result is attached to that message.
- **A message can carry a roll that has not happened yet**, which is a different shape from carrying a
  result. It belongs in the message model from the start, because retrofitting it changes the type.
- A clickable roll acted on by a reader becomes an ordinary roll under R-2.2: their own client
  produces it, attributed to them.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.14** A clickable roll acted on by a reader produces a roll from **that reader's** client,
  attributed to them. A build in which the author's client produces it, or which attributes it to the
  author, fails.

### R-2.9 Buttons, and who may press them

- **A clickable roll is open to everyone by default.**
- **A DM may address one to named players.** Everyone else sees it greyed, and who it is for: they know
  it exists and that it is not theirs. Targeting is a protocol field.
- **Visible-but-greyed is the requirement, not hidden.** A button that vanishes for non-addressees
  makes the table disagree about what the DM said.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.28** A button addressed to named players is visible and greyed to everyone else, not hidden.
  A build that hides it fails, and passes any check asking only whether non-addressees can press it.

### R-2.10 Missed messages

**Messages missed while a member is dropped are delivered on reconnect, within the seat window**
(session-layer R-1.5a, product-overview D-17).

- **A gap that could not be held is marked in that member's stream.** They are told something is
  missing rather than shown a stream that silently omits it.
- **Delivery on reconnect re-sends.** A build that restores a reconnecting member's stream by re-sending
  what it missed conforms; this is what the requirement asks for.
- **This is not history for newcomers.** A client receives nothing from before its admission: a late
  joiner gets no earlier messages or rolls, and a relink is a new admission on a new key and starts
  empty (product-overview Session panel, "Log history is never sent"). This covers only a member who
  held a seat and missed traffic while holding it; the seat is the entitlement.
- Safe only under R-2.6; see the coupling there.

**What makes a gap unholdable.**

- **A bound exists; its value is engineering's.** The host cannot hold unbounded traffic, and how much
  to hold is a resource judgement. Where the bound lives (inside the holding type or at its caller) is
  also engineering's.
- **There is a product floor, and it is the half that matters.** Marking is the exception clause. The
  feature is "you get your messages back", not "you are told you did not"; a build where marking is
  the ordinary outcome implements this in name only.
- **So a drop at the short end of the seat window must not produce a gap where a drop at the long end
  may.** Stated relatively on purpose: it needs no constant, and it fails a decorative build that
  holds a handful of messages and gaps at both ends.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.6** A client receives nothing from before its admission. A late joiner is shown no earlier
  messages or rolls. This is not the reconnect case (A-2.6a).
- **A-2.6a** A member that reconnects inside the seat window receives what it missed, and a gap that
  could not be held is marked. Both halves fail separately: delivering nothing fails, and delivering
  silently past a gap fails.
- **A-2.10a** A short drop does not gap. Drop a member briefly in an active session and reconnect well
  inside the seat window: they receive what they missed, with no gap marker. Drop one at the window's
  far edge: a marker there is permitted. A build that gaps at both ends fails. No constant is asserted.
  Tests that prove the marking mechanism marks when told to are not evidence that any real drop ever
  gaps; whoever writes the caller that first applies a bound re-runs the marking checks against that
  caller. Needs two machines.

### R-2.11 Moderation

**The DM may delete anything from the log, and a marker stays where it was.**

- **This is not anti-cheat and must not be built as though it were.** Its purpose is the opposite:
  the log must never silently disagree with what people watched happen.
- A deletion that leaves no trace is the failure, because it makes the record differ from the memory
  of everyone who was there.
- The marker records **that something was removed and by whom**, not what it said.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.26** A deleted message leaves a marker in the same place on every client. Both halves fail
  separately: a deletion leaving no trace fails, and a marker in a different position on two clients
  fails.

### R-2.12 Retention and export

**Every client records its own log: the stream it received.** What differs between the DM's log and a
player's is retention, not recording.

- Recording is not authoring. The host authors the shared stream and its order; each client writes
  down what arrived. A player's client inventing entries the host never sent would violate
  product-overview D-3; one recording what it received does not.
- **The host's log is a per-client log too**: what the DM's client received, not an assembled
  superset. The stream's lifetime, owner and construction site are engineering's.
- **A player's log dies with the session unless exported.**
- **The DM's client retains its log automatically**, on their machine, where campaign data lives.
- **A retained log is deletable from the same place as everything else**: Campaign storage in
  settings, with its campaign (session-layer R-1.6). A retained log that no delete control reaches is
  data the DM cannot clear.
- **A roll log is not campaign data.** A campaign persists a roster, which is metadata; a log is what
  people said and did. Moving the player side into the campaign store is a product decision, not a
  storage one.
- **Export is an act, never a setting or a default.** The DM's retained log is not an export
  (session-layer A-1.11a-note).
- **The export is offered at session end, not buried in a menu.** A keep-or-lose choice presented after
  the thing is gone is not a choice.
  - What binds is that **the log survives until the choice resolves.** Blocking teardown is one way to
    arrange that and is not required.
  - **The product does not wait unboundedly for an answer** (session-layer R-1.3c): the log outlives
    the offer's window, and the window ends. How (a modal, a deferred prompt, a hold on one object) is
    engineering's.
  - **An ignored offer applies the default:** the player's log dies with the session. That is not a
    silent discard (session-layer A-1.2z), because the offer is what makes it not silent; declining
    by inaction is declining.
- **An export contains only what its owner could see.** This follows from each client recording what
  it received: a participant not entitled to a result never receives it (product-overview D-13,
  R-2.15), so it was never in their log. **No export may reconstruct a view its owner never had**, and
  "merge the logs so the DM gets the full picture" is forbidden.
- **An export names nobody** (product-overview D-20). No participant name and no participant
  identifier, in any field, however scoped. **Authorship is carried by a label local to that one
  file**, assigned at write time from the file's own contents and derived from no stored or
  transmitted value. Every other candidate for an author column is already excluded: display name
  (session-layer A-1.2a; it defaults to the character name, so it would put FFXIV character names
  in the file for every player who never set an alias), peer code (session-layer A-1.11c) and
  participant id (session-layer A-1.11a).
  - **No legend, ever**: no field for names, and no way to fill one in, at export, afterwards, or as a
    setting. A legend turns D-8's guarantee from a property into a default.
  - **The file says its labels mean nothing outside it**, and not that the files cannot be related.
    Two exports of one session can be aligned on host time and their labels partially mapped, so the
    stronger sentence is false. Decision 13's "two exports agree" (product-overview Session panel
    item 13) is about order and time, not attribution.
  - **What reopens this:** an export format gaining a field that persists across different sessions
    (a campaign label, a stable code). The label is safe because it joins to nothing; a joinable
    neighbour makes it joinable in combination. Adding any field to the export format means
    re-checking it against D-20.
  - The DM's retained log is not reached by this bullet: it may hold names and peer codes
    (product-overview D-8).
- **An accepted offer never presents an act the build does not perform.** An accept that writes
  nothing, or that writes the retained-log format (which carries peer codes), is worse than no accept.
  Either the export is actually produced, or accept is not offered while it cannot be honoured, or the
  offer says **at the offer** that the export cannot be written (product-overview D-18). Release notes
  and settings copy do not satisfy this: the false belief forms at the moment of choosing and the log
  is gone a second later.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.16** An export is a function of exactly one log and cannot take two. An exporter that can be
  handed two logs, or that reaches for a second, fails. Owner-scoping itself is entailed by A-2.15 and
  is not this row's work.
- **A-2.16a** The absence is a two-client observation. Run a DM-private roll on one machine and inspect
  the other client's log: the entry is absent. It cannot be checked by constructing an entry the
  player should not have, since under D-13 that value is not constructible on their client. A build
  that holds it and filters at export fails A-2.15 first.
- **A-2.17** Export is never automatic. Run a session and end it; no exported file exists until an
  export is invoked. The DM's retained log is not an export (A-2.22).
- **A-2.17a** An export names nobody. Export a session and assert that no display name, no peer code
  and no participant id appears anywhere in the artefact. **Plant each forbidden value in turn and
  require the check to go red**; a search over an export that never held a name returns the same clean
  zero as a conforming one. Entries are attributed by a file-local label. An identifier is a value
  that can be joined; a write-time label exists only inside its file.
- **A-2.17b** No legend, and both halves fail separately: the exported format carries no field for
  names, **and** the export flow offers no way to fill one in (not at export, not afterwards, not as a
  setting). A nameless format with a settings box that writes names into it fails the second half.
- **A-2.17c** The file says its labels are file-local, at the right strength. Both halves fail
  separately: an absent statement fails, because two exports of one session produce labels that do not
  correspond and an unlabelled ordinal silently misleads anyone cross-referencing them; **and
  overclaiming fails**: the wording is "these labels mean nothing outside this file", never "these
  files cannot be related" (session-layer R-1.7a).
- **A-2.21** A retained log is deletable from Campaign storage in settings. Retain a log, use that
  control, and assert the log is gone.
- **A-2.22** A player's log does not survive the session unless exported; the DM's does, without being
  asked. Both halves fail separately.
- **A-2.23** The export is offered at session end. A build where the only route is a settings menu
  fails.
- **A-2.23a** Accepting the session-end offer does not present an act the build does not perform. A
  player accepts and no export is produced: fails, unless the offer said at the offer that the export
  cannot be written. An accept that writes a file carrying a participant identifier fails A-2.17a.
  Both halves fail separately. The writes-nothing half is machine-checkable; the wording needs the
  game.

### R-2.13 Following the session in FFXIV

**The echo is on by default, for rolls and membership events only.** Narration and conversation stay in
the panel unless the player switches them on. The default has two halves and a build can fail either:
echoing nothing by default fails, and echoing everything by default fails.

- The reason: **the panel is for acting, not watching.** A player reads the game and should not have to
  keep a second window in view to follow their table. Mechanics are terse and are the part worth never
  missing; dialogue would dominate a main chat log that has no tab to contain it.
- **The plugin never writes the player's game UI configuration** (product-overview D-1): not by
  default, not on first run, and not yet as an opt-in. The only way to isolate plugin output into its
  own tab is to rename and refilter one of the player's two configurable tabs, which appropriates a
  scarce resource the player owns. An opt-in is not offered yet, because a consent dialog for a
  mechanism nobody has executed promises something the product cannot keep. An opt-in may follow once
  the mechanism has been run in the game, not on the strength of reading its API.
- **The echo prints with a chat type the player's own filter UI can select**, so a player can build
  their own tab with the game's UI. Otherwise "you can make your own tab" is advice the product cannot
  honour.
- **A gap marker echoes.** A marker that does not reach the reader is not a marker: without it, a player
  following in game chat gets an unmarked hole, the defect R-2.10 exists to prevent. It is terse, rare,
  and about the reader's own continuity.
- **A contentless notice always echoes, whatever its subject, including a private message.** The
  notice is not a reduced form of its subject; it is what remains when content is withheld, a pointer
  rather than a message. Without it, a private message from the DM reaches a player following in game
  chat nowhere at all. One line for a rare event is not what the volume argument is about.
- **The panel remains the source of truth.** The echo renders what the client already received. It is
  never a send, and nothing here drives the game's chat-send path (product-overview D-1). The echo is
  never the only place a roll appears.
- `Character (Player)` travels into the echo (R-2.7), and so does the host marker (R-2.7a).

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.9** With the echo on, a session event in its default scope appears in the player's game chat;
  with the echo off, nothing is written there.
- **A-2.9b** The echo prints with a chat type that appears as a selectable option in the game's
  chat-filter UI. A build printing with a type the player cannot filter on fails. Needs the game
  running.
- **A-2.9c** The plugin writes no game UI configuration by any route: no setting of any UI
  configuration option by default, not on first run, not behind a setting, not behind a consent
  dialog. A build offering the player a choice to let it do so fails while the mechanism has not been
  run in the game, because an opt-in for a mechanism nobody has executed is a promise the product
  cannot keep. An opt-in offered after that run is R-2.13's reopen, not a failure of this criterion.
- **A-2.9d** By default, rolls and membership events echo, and narration and conversation do not. **On a
  fresh install with nothing configured**, a roll and a join/leave produce echo lines; a narration line
  and a **public** chat message do not. Public matters: a private message does produce an echo line
  (its notice, A-2.9g). Both halves fail separately. The fresh-install condition matters: a build with
  the right toggles and the wrong defaults passes every test that configures before observing.
- **A-2.9e** Narration and conversation can be switched on, and then they echo. A build with no such
  control fails, and so does one whose control is present and inert.
- **A-2.9f** A gap marker echoes. Drop and reconnect a member so R-2.10 marks a gap, and follow the
  session in game chat: the marker is there. A panel marker with an unmarked hole in the echo fails.
  Needs two machines.
- **A-2.9g** A contentless notice echoes whatever its subject. Send a DM-private message to a player
  following in game chat: a contentless notice appears there. A build where it does not fails. Needs
  two machines.
- **A-2.9g-1** Switching conversation on never turns the notice into its contents. Enable conversation
  echo (A-2.9e), then send a DM-private message: the echo is still the contentless notice. No setting
  reaches R-2.14. Needs two machines.

### R-2.14 Private messages never echo

**The contents of a private message never reach FFXIV's chat log. A contentless notice only**
("a private message from the DM", "your blind roll was sent").

- FFXIV's chat log is outside the product's control: it may be persisted to disk and read by other
  plugins. Content sealed per recipient is not handed to a store the product does not govern.
- A notice that something private arrived is permitted; what it said is not. This does not depend on
  the echo scope in R-2.13, and no setting changes it.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.29** A private message's content never reaches FFXIV's chat log. Send one and inspect what was
  written to the game: a contentless notice passes; any part of the content fails.

### R-2.15 Roll modes

| Mode | Who sees the result |
| --- | --- |
| **Public** | Everyone in the session. The default. |
| **Private to the DM** | The roller and the DM. |
| **Blind** | The DM only in the UI. Not enforced; see below. |

- There is no Self mode (roller only) and no player-to-player targeting, because player-to-player
  privacy is not built (R-2.6).
- Public and DM-private are **access levels under product-overview D-13**: a participant who may not see
  a result never receives one, rather than being shown a hidden UI.
- **A reveal is an action someone takes, not an absence.** A result hidden forever is a lost roll, and
  the log records that it was revealed rather than silently changing.
- **Blind is not enforced, and the product does not claim otherwise.** Under R-2.2 the roller's own
  client produced the value. Blind hides a number in the UI and sends it to the DM; that is the whole
  mechanism, and it is sufficient (product-overview "No defence against a participant cheating their
  own table").
- **Copy may not call a blind roll secret, private, hidden from everyone, or secure**
  (session-layer R-1.7a). It need not advertise that it is defeatable. Say what it does, not what it
  withstands.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.12** Blind's copy says what it does and claims nothing about what it withstands. Copy calling a
  blind roll secret, private, hidden from everyone, or secure fails. Copy that simply does not mention
  how it could be circumvented does not fail.
- **A-2.13** A blind roll's value **is present** on the roller's client, and a DM-private roll's value
  **is absent** from every client not entitled to it. Both halves fail separately. Withholding the
  blind value from the roller would mean the host rolled, violating R-2.2; asserting only the second
  half passes a build that treats both modes the same.

### R-2.16 The product does not defend against a participant cheating their own table

"Rolls being manipulated is going to take a lot of effort, and at that point the campaign as a whole
has failed, not the plugin." So: **no anti-cheat, no tamper detection, no host-authoritative rolls,
and no cryptography aimed at stopping a player learning something their own client already knows**
(product-overview "No defence against a participant cheating their own table").

- **The UI never claims rolls are verified or tamper-proof.**
- **The scope is narrow.** This says nothing about the relay, the operator, or anyone outside the
  session. Session encryption still assumes a hostile relay (product-overview D-11) and the operator is
  still kept out (product-overview D-8). This is only about the people the table invited; citing it to
  justify weaker session encryption is a misreading.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.11** No UI copy anywhere describes rolls as verified, fair, tamper-proof or server-generated.

### R-2.17 Display names: persistence, scoped to a campaign

The choice of display name is session-layer R-1.3e. It is what R-2.7's parenthetical renders, and it
never authenticates (product-overview D-8). How a name appears beside a message or roll is R-2.7.

**A name a player chose is offered back to them, so they are not retyping it every session.**
Per-campaign is the requirement, not a convenience: identity is campaign-scoped and never portable
(product-overview D-8).

- **A chosen name persists within a campaign.**
- **It does not follow the player into a different campaign.** A name that did would be a portable
  identifier arriving through a convenience.
- **There is no global "remember my name" setting**, because that is the same thing with a friendlier
  surface. The risk is not failing to build persistence; it is building it globally, which is simpler
  and forbidden.

**A name stored before campaign scoping becomes a local pre-fill default.** Not migrated, not
dropped, not asked about at upgrade: an upgrade-time prompt would ask the player to decide about
campaign-scoped names before they have met the concept.

- This survives D-8 only because **the stored value is a local default and never travels as itself.**
  What reaches the wire is what the player accepted for that campaign. A pre-fill source that is never
  sent is a convenience; the same bytes sent everywhere would be an identifier.
- **Acceptance is an act.** A pre-filled field the player never saw never silently becomes their
  campaign name (session-layer R-1.3e, A-1.2g). "Never silently assign" is the twin of "never silently
  discard" (session-layer A-1.2z).

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.10** A player can set a display name different from their character name, and other
  participants see the chosen name. Needs two machines.
- **A-2.30** A chosen display name is offered again in the same campaign and is not offered in a
  different one. Choose a name in campaign A and return to A: it is offered. Host campaign B: it is
  not. Both halves fail separately; the second is the one a build gets wrong.
- **A-2.31** No setting, export or store persists a display name outside a campaign. A global "remember
  my name" option fails even if unset, because the criterion is about what the product can do. **One
  exception: the carried-over pre-fill default**, exactly one value, whose only permitted reader is the
  pre-fill path, and which never travels as itself (A-2.32). A second globally stored name-shaped
  value fails, and so does any reader of the first outside the pre-fill path.
- **A-2.31a** A-2.31 is checked against **what is persisted**: read the persisted document and find
  exactly one globally stored name-shaped value (the carry-over) and no other. A build adding a second
  fails; a build with none also fails, because the carry-over must exist. Comparing freshly constructed
  settings objects cannot catch a name-shaped field added to settings.
- **A-2.32** The carried-over default never travels as itself. Hold a stored pre-fill default, join two
  campaigns, and inspect what leaves the client: what is sent is what the player accepted in that
  campaign. A build sending the stored default fails even if the bytes match what the player would
  have accepted; the criterion is over provenance, not the string.
- **A-2.33** A pre-filled name the player never saw is never sent. Carry a default into a new campaign
  and complete a join without the name ever being displayed: nothing is sent under that name. A build
  that pre-fills correctly and sends without showing passes A-2.32 and fails this. The send is
  machine-checkable; the player having seen it needs the game.

### R-2.18 Invoking a roll

- **A roll is invoked by a command** ("a dice command, like `/roll 1d20+2` or something like that").
- **The token is a condition, not a word: the one a tabletop player tries first that does not shadow a
  game command.** `/roll` is today's answer. If the two ever conflict, the condition governs and the
  word changes. A command token is taught, repeated and written into other people's notes and macros,
  so it is less revisable than it looks.
- **No `/r` alias, ever.** Foundry supports `/r` and it will be the obvious thing to add, but in FFXIV
  `/r` is reply: a player typing `/r` to answer a tell would send it somewhere else, possibly in front
  of the table.
- **Conditional on the surface.** If the token is ever registered as a game-wide command, that `/roll`
  is free in FFXIV is verified first, and if it is taken the ruling changes. If it is only typed into
  the product's own input, the question does not arise.
- The input surface (slash command, window control, chat input) is not specified here. Base chat comes
  first in the build order (R-2.19), and the invoker sits behind it.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.33a** `/roll <expression>` invokes a roll, and `/r` is claimed in no way. Both halves fail
  separately: type `/roll 1d20` and a roll happens; type `/r` and the product claims it in no way at all
  (no handler, no alias entry, no completion suggestion). A build that never registers `/r` but never
  invokes on `/roll` passes the second half vacuously.
- **A-2.33b** A-2.33a tests the word because a condition about what a player tries first cannot be run.
  If the condition and the word conflict, R-2.18 governs, the word changes, and A-2.33a changes with
  it; the token is not changed by editing the criterion alone. This row cannot fail.
- **A-2.33c** If `/roll` is registered as a game-wide command, it does not shadow an existing FFXIV
  command, checked in the game before it ships. Vacuous while the token is typed only into the
  product's own input.

### R-2.19 Base chat: a member can say something

- **A member composes a message and sends it to the session. Every admitted member receives it.**
- **The send path is reachable from the running product.** A path closed inside the core library, with
  no route a person running the plugin can take, does not satisfy this: its subject is a member
  composing, and the build order's words are "people in the session can interact". No screen is named;
  a slash command, a window control or a chat input all satisfy it.
- **A message is untrusted input and is bounded**, length at minimum. **A message exceeding a bound is
  refused with a message naming the fault, not silently truncated** (session-layer A-1.2v's rule, one
  layer over). The bound values are engineering's; that bounds exist is not. A message crosses the same
  wire from the same strangers as a roll expression (R-2.1a).
- **Inbound, the bound is enforced at the receiving client's boundary**, and a frame refused there is
  this requirement working. What the receiver owes is product-overview D-22: **the refusal is recorded
  locally, never attributed to anyone, never rendered on a session surface, bounded however many
  refusals arrive, and the refused content is never kept in whole or in part.** A record of the user's
  own client's behaviour is a diagnostic and is required; a record that accuses another participant is
  moderation and is not permitted. The likeliest cause of a refused frame is version skew
  (product-overview D-14), not hostility; a peer flooding refused frames is for the relay operator's
  rate-limiting, not the DM. No marker appears in the stream, because nobody watched the frame arrive:
  a marker would manufacture a disagreement and hand its timing to the sender (R-2.11).
- This adds the producer only. Message kinds, targets, speaker, ordering, retention and echo are
  R-2.3 through R-2.14 and are not restated.

**Acceptance criteria**
- **A-2.34** A **non-host** member sends a message and every **other** admitted member receives it.
  Assert it arrives at a different member, not only on the sender's own screen; local echo passes a
  build that never reached the wire, and a build where only the host can originate passes every other
  message criterion.
- **A-2.35** An over-long message is refused with the fault named: not truncated, not dropped. Three
  outcomes and one passes: silent truncation fails (the sender believes they said something they did
  not), a silent drop fails, and a freeze or out-of-memory fails this requirement. No bound value is
  asserted; crossing it is visible to the person who crossed it. **This is the sender's side.** On an
  inbound path the person who crossed the bound is on another machine, so this row does not govern a
  receiver; A-2.36 through A-2.40 do.
- **A-2.36** An inbound refusal is recorded at all; silence fails. Drive a message past the inbound
  bound and assert a local record of the refusal exists. This is asserted first and separately,
  because A-2.37 through A-2.40 are all negatives that a build recording nothing satisfies perfectly.
  **The record is written on the refusal path and distinguishes a refusal from an acceptance:** a
  counter that increments on every arrival is evidence of an arrival, not a refusal, and makes this row
  unfailable. **The record itself satisfies A-2.40:** reusing a store that holds the refused content to
  prove the fact fails. Form, location and retention are engineering's.
- **A-2.37** The record names nobody. In everything a person can read, no display name, peer code or
  participant id appears. **Plant each forbidden value into the refused frame in turn** and require the
  search to find it in the frame and not in the record; a search over an empty record returns the same
  clean zero as a conforming one.
- **A-2.38** Nothing reaches a session surface. After a refusal, the message stream and the roster are
  unchanged: no entry, no marker, no gap indicator. Asserted together with A-2.36's record, because an
  unchanged stream is also what a build where the refusal never happened shows.
- **A-2.39** The record cannot be grown by whoever caused it. Drive N refusals and 10N refusals and
  assert the record's footprint is bounded by the same value in both, on screen, in logs and on disk. A
  per-refusal entry with no cap fails even though each entry is small. No number is asserted.
- **A-2.40** The refused content is absent from the record, in whole or in part. Plant a distinctive
  sentinel in the refused payload and assert it appears nowhere in the record, displayed or stored. A
  log line carrying a truncated prefix "for diagnostics" fails; a whole-payload equality check would
  miss it. Show the search finds the sentinel in the refused frame, or the absence is a fact about the
  search.
- **A-2.36-note** A-2.36 through A-2.40 cover the inbound message bound only, the discarding path that
  exists today. product-overview D-22 reaches every path that discards received input, in the client
  and in the relay (an undecryptable message, a malformed frame, a document the client will not load).
  Each such path gets its own five criteria when it is built.
- **A-2.41** A member can reach the send path from the running product. Something outside the
  session/message layer invokes it: a call from the product's own composition root or its window layer,
  not from a test. A-2.34 cannot detect this, since a test harness calling the API satisfies "send
  from a member". **The check stops at the last hop the product authors:** it does not require a
  third-party framework honouring its own documented contract to be measured, but it does cover every
  hop this repository writes, including registering its own dispatcher with the framework, and each
  such registration points at a target whose existence the compiler enforces (for example an
  `override` of a base member). A build where a person running the plugin has no route to sending
  fails. Checked as a reference/call-graph assertion, not a UI test.

## Out of scope

- **Reading game chat, in any form, for any purpose** (A-2.7). No chat-message subscription. A feature
  that seems to need one is a product decision (product-overview non-goals).
- **Sending anything into the game** (product-overview D-1). The echo is a local render, never a send.
- **Rules content** (product-overview D-4). Dice arithmetic is in scope; knowing what a roll means is
  not.
- **Success/failure resolution.** Deferred, not a non-goal. No target-number concept is built, and the
  grammar must not introduce one as a side effect.
- **Player-to-player privacy.** Deliberate; see R-2.6.
- Initiative ordering belongs to the initiative area, even though initiative is rolled here.
- **Dice animation** is wanted and deferred, so it does not arrive as polish.

## Open questions

- Open question: does an over-large keep/drop count (one exceeding the pool) clamp or refuse under the
  adopted grammar? No Foundry instance is available to run, and the documentation's in-pool examples
  say nothing about out-of-pool behaviour, so inferring from `20d20kh10` to `2d6dl20` would be a guess.
  It blocks A-2.3b for `4d6dl9` and `2d6dl20` only. `4d6kh0`, `4d6dl4` and `4d6dl4+100` (keep of zero,
  or drop count equal to the pool) do not depend on it and fail A-2.3b today if the result is silent.
- Open question: what should a modifier followed by a tab, newline, CR or NBSP do? Only the space case
  is established (A-2.3c); the others are unspecified.
- Open question: is `/roll` free in FFXIV? Believed to be (the game uses `/random` and `/dice`), not
  verified. It blocks registering the token as a game-wide command (R-2.18, A-2.33c); it blocks
  nothing while the token is typed only into the product's own input.
- Open question: R-2.10 is not built. Nothing holds messages for a dropped member, and nothing marks a
  gap (the stream has a gap kind that nothing produces). A dropped member comes back only by relink,
  which is a new admission and starts empty, so A-2.6a and A-2.10a fail today. Whether the seat
  window may serve as the bound R-2.10 requires is left to whoever builds it.
- Open question: chat commands as an input path were raised by the user and are not wanted; they are
  recorded only as raised. Whether they are even possible, given product-overview D-1 (no driving the
  chat-send path) and A-2.7 (no chat-read subscription), is unassessed. Not to be closed by building
  it.
- Open question: should rolls be attributable to a character distinct from the participant, for a DM
  running several NPCs? The speaker field (R-2.7) largely answers it; whether several NPC speakers can
  be held at once is not decided.
- Open question: the Foundry ChatLog and ChatMessages references (see Reference) have not been analysed.
  Whether sidebar behaviour, filtering, paging or in-memory retention need requirements here is
  undecided.
